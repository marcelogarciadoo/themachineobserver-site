From raw evidence to a testable forecast: the proposed logic, step by step.

Why examine corrections?

Some historical misses are too large for the published sampling margin to describe total polling uncertainty. In the final 2022 first-round Datafolha release, Bolsonaro had 36% of valid votes; the official result was 43.20%: a 7.20-point underestimation, versus a published ±2-point survey margin. This is a documented example, not the average error of all institutes or proof of permanent bias. Final poll · TSE result.

Population assumptions can be outdated or incompatible with the electorate being measured. IBGE reweighted PNAD Contínua in 2025 using population projections that incorporate the 2022 Census. An Ipsos-Ipec release from December 2025 declared Census 2022 and PNAD 2023 benchmarks. That difference in vintage warrants a wave-by-wave audit; it does not establish that the institute’s 2026 estimates are inaccurate. IBGE · Ipsos-Ipec.

Nonresponse, coverage, question wording, interview mode, turnout and late decisions can also matter. Demographic reweighting is not a cure for all of them. Quaest itself describes political-geographic sampling and likely-voter considerations beyond demographic quotas. We will accept corrections only when their inputs are identifiable and historical validation supports them. Quaest methodology.

Current status: a Datafolha demographic research reconstruction is now running. It covers ten 2026 runoff waves and five benchmarkable variables. 01 · Define the target

Specify national scope, election/round and exact candidate scenario. Keep three different quantities separate: a published poll, current opinion and election-day vote share. Retain all-respondent and valid-vote denominators separately. The runoff target is Lula versus Flávio; a market contract price is neither vote share nor an input to this model.

02 · Preserve the evidence

Archive each report and retrieval time with a SHA-256 hash. Record publication and fieldwork dates, sample, mode, geography, tested list, question wording, published margin/confidence level and registration. Identify the institute separately from its sponsor. Registration in PesqEle is not certification that the estimate is accurate. Track missing first-round results against every corresponding runoff wave.

03 · Check and normalize

One independent wave is not several independent observations merely because several articles or candidate scenarios were published. Preserve original percentages and a revision log; never manufacture zeroes or silently force rounded totals to 100. Check dates, geography and denominator before comparison. Flag inconsistent sources for review. Keep spontaneous questions separate from stimulated candidate lists.

04 · Show the raw baseline

For each candidate and day t, average available published results dated t−6 through t, inclusively, with one equal-weight observation per wave. Missing candidate results are excluded, not zero. Empty statistical windows remain missing. For visual continuity only, a dotted segment holds the last available average until new evidence arrives; there is no backfill before the first observation, no invented poll and no future information. Institute filters apply before averaging; period filters retain the preceding six-day lookback. First-round scenario composition can change: this is a descriptive mixed-scenario summary, not a comparable-electorate model. The Flávio−Lula line averages paired within-poll differences. Published margins apply only to raw points, not to the average. Markets receive no moving average.

05 · Audit demographic inputs

For each wave, inspect its declared population base, vintage and weighting variables. IBGE resident-population estimates, the TSE registered electorate and actual voters are different universes; do not substitute one for another. Match age cutoffs, territory, education and income definitions. Reconcile census/PNAD revisions and their publication dates. A newer PNAD does not prove an older poll is biased, and demographics cannot alone identify politically selective nonresponse.

06 · Choose an admissible correction

No usable cross-tabs: leave the poll unadjusted and document the gap. With complete compatible one-way profiles and matching vote tables, the current Datafolha research model starts from a 360-cell PNAD sex × age × education × income × region joint distribution, rakes it to current TSE/PNAD targets and to each published poll profile, fits one maximum-entropy main-effects response surface to all tables simultaneously, and poststratifies once. It never adds five marginal corrections. Shapley allocation then averages each variable’s incremental contribution over all 120 correction orders so the parts reconcile exactly to the joint change. This is a model-dependent public-table reconstruction, not recovered respondent weights; microdata remain preferable. Rounding and alternative dependency structures are reported as sensitivity, and unsupported polls remain missing rather than receiving a zero correction.

07 · Estimate current opinion jointly

Fit individual waves, not their moving averages as additional observations. The executed research model applies ten evidence-gated Datafolha demographic reconstructions, leaves every unsupported wave raw, and jointly estimates a piecewise-linear current trend with partially pooled institute effects. Effects are constrained to average zero and are residual standardizations, not causal proof of pollster bias. Mode is not separately identifiable in the normalized inputs. Every poll stores raw value, demographic status, residual effect and standardized value separately.

08 · Replay historical elections

The executed replay rebuilds 60-, 30-, 14-, 7- and 3-day cutoffs using only polls published by each cutoff. Twelve cutoffs across the 2018 runoff and both 2022 rounds have sufficient institute coverage. The 2018 first-round top-pair outcome is now reconciled for model use, but only four eligible polls remain, so it cannot score the residual architecture. No 2026 demographic adjustment is injected into history: comparable as-of crosstabs are unavailable.

09 · Validate or reject

The frozen point model reduced mean absolute margin error from 5.58 to 4.97 points, but its narrow current-state 95% intervals covered only 33% of eventual outcomes, failing the 80% gate. A separate leave-one-election-round-out predictive calibration learns each held-out interval width only from the other contests and reaches 83% coverage across 12 cutoffs. This repairs election-day interval calibration, not the missing historical PNAD test: with only three independent contests, both adjusted estimates and probabilities remain research-only.

10 · Freeze the current estimate

Freeze inputs, parameters and current-opinion uncertainty at the publication cutoff. The research ledger shows each institute’s latest raw runoff margin beside its demographic component, residual standardization and available total. Because demographic evidence is unavailable for most waves, most totals are explicitly partial. A future leave-one-institute-out distance ranking will require a validated benchmark at the same date, scenario and denominator; the present correction-size ranking is not that accuracy claim. Historical outcome errors remain a separate table.

11 · Model voting and late change

The executed Step 5 component ledger records official turnout and invalid-vote rates from the 2018 and 2022 runoffs, the current unresolved response pool, long-horizon replay errors and the limits of aggregate data. Turnout composition and unresolved-voter allocation are not identifiable from public marginal tables, so the base model invents no directional adjustment. Alternative assumptions remain versioned in the research artifact for audit, but the main results page presents only the calibrated base distribution and its predictive intervals.

12 · Run correlated Monte Carlo draws

The research simulation runs 50,000 seeded draws in log-odds space, keeping Lula and Flávio complementary on a valid-vote basis. It combines current-estimate uncertainty with a conservative future-error scale learned from 30- and 60-day historical replays. The base future mean shift and current/future correlation remain zero because the archive cannot identify them credibly. The Predict victory chart uses 100 one-percentage-point bins—twice the previous histogram resolution—on a 30%–70% horizontal scale. It marks the simulation mean and overlays a Gaussian fitted from the simulated mean and standard deviation as a descriptive reference; the curve does not replace the empirical Monte Carlo distribution or assert Gaussian model errors. This remains a conditional runoff model, not an unconditional presidency forecast.

13 · Summarize the distribution

The local research pages now report the mean simulated valid-vote share, 50%, 80% and 95% predictive intervals, and conditional win probability separately. The intervals describe election-day uncertainty and are much wider than a poll sampling margin. Seed, draw count, cutoff, model version and simulation error are stored in the release artifact. None of these values is approved for the production site.

14 · Publish an auditable version

Publish the cutoff/timezone, input IDs/hashes, benchmark vintages, code/configuration versions, parameter estimates, seed/draw count, exclusions, validation report and change log. Keep raw evidence, transformations, model output and presentation separate. Preserve previous releases rather than overwriting the historical record. After official results, score the frozen forecasts and use lessons only in clearly versioned future revisions. Kalshi and Polymarket retain their own provider timestamps, contract definitions and settlement rules, outside every correction, model fit and simulation.

How the offenders ranking is calculated

For each certified election round, select the latest eligible poll of total respondents published within 14 days of the vote. Calculate the poll margin as the opponent share minus the Lula or Haddad share and compare it with the same official-result margin. Take the absolute difference, in percentage points, then average those absolute errors across the available election rounds for each institute. Rank the resulting institute means from largest to smallest. The main chart uses this total error only: it does not subtract the published candidate margin of error and does not mix PNAD or residual components into the historical outcome comparison. Event counts remain visible because institutes with one or two eligible rounds have highly uncertain rankings. Open the ranking and evidence table.
